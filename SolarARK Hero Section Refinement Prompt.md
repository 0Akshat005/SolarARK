## SolarARK Hero Section — Premium UI/UX Refinement

Use the attached current SolarARK homepage screenshot as the visual reference and refine the existing hero section in the implementation.

Do **not** redesign the website from scratch. Do **not** replace the overall concept, brand identity, typography direction, navigation structure, or current hero image yet. The goal of this task is to professionally correct the subtle composition, hierarchy, spacing, readability, and conversion issues that currently make the hero feel slightly unfinished.

Think like a senior product designer and conversion-focused frontend developer creating a premium Indian residential solar brand.

### 1. Preserve the current visual direction

Keep the existing:
- SolarARK branding and logo
- primary blue brand language
- headline wording
- CTA wording
- overall navigation structure
- current hero image
- clean, optimistic, family-oriented positioning

Do not introduce unnecessary gradients, glassmorphism, excessive cards, dark sections, or generic SaaS styling.

The finished section should feel like a **premium residential solar company**, not a template.

---

### 2. Improve the hero image treatment

The current photograph is good, but the webpage currently appears to place content over the image rather than composing the image around the content.

Improve the image treatment through CSS/layout only:

- Position the hero image so the rooftop solar system and home remain visually dominant on the right.
- Prevent the house from becoming excessively large or visually crowding the content.
- Keep the solar panels clearly visible.
- Create a softer visual transition toward the left side so the headline remains naturally readable.
- Replace the current overly obvious bottom fade with a much more refined transition into the next section.
- Avoid making the image look washed out.
- Preserve realistic photographic contrast and warmth.
- Do not apply heavy blur to the entire image.
- Do not put a generic dark overlay over the full image.

The image should feel **cinematic but realistic**, with the content integrated naturally into the composition.

---

### 3. Fix the left-side content hierarchy

The current headline is strong, but the content stack feels slightly too conventional.

Refine the spacing and hierarchy so the eye naturally follows:

**Headline → supporting message → primary CTA → secondary action → trust signal**

Use stronger vertical rhythm.

The headline should remain the main visual anchor.

Keep:

**Power your home.**  
**Pay less every month.**

Do not rewrite the messaging.

Make sure:
- line breaks feel intentional
- headline width is controlled
- blue emphasis remains visually balanced
- supporting text doesn't sit too close to the headline
- CTA group feels connected to the headline
- trust statement feels like reassurance rather than another separate section

Avoid excessive spacing that makes the content look vertically disconnected.

---

### 4. Improve CTA presentation

Keep the existing primary CTA:

**Get My Free Savings Estimate →**

Make it feel more premium through:
- refined height
- balanced horizontal padding
- subtle shadow
- excellent border radius
- strong typography
- precise hover state
- consistent alignment with the content column

The CTA must look clickable immediately without becoming oversized.

Keep the secondary:

**See How It Works ↗**

but visually reduce its competition with the primary CTA.

The primary CTA must clearly win the hierarchy.

Under or near the primary CTA, introduce a very subtle reassurance line if the design allows:

**Takes about 60 seconds • No obligation**

Do this only if it can be integrated elegantly without clutter.

---

### 5. Strengthen trust messaging

The current trust statement is useful, but its visual treatment feels slightly detached.

Refine it into one coherent trust element.

Keep the concept:

**Trusted by homeowners across Maharashtra.**  
**Quality installation. Government subsidy support.**

Make the icon, typography and spacing feel like a single reassurance unit.

The trust element should visually communicate:

**“This company is established, local, and safe to consider.”**

Do not turn it into a large badge/card.

---

### 6. Improve navigation over the hero

The navbar currently competes slightly with the photograph.

Refine it subtly:

- Improve contrast against the background.
- Maintain the clean transparent/over-image appearance.
- Add a very subtle backdrop treatment only if needed for readability.
- Do not make it look like a large glassmorphism panel.
- Keep navigation airy.
- Maintain excellent spacing between menu items.
- Make the phone number and icon feel secondary to the primary CTA.

The navbar should support the hero rather than becoming another competing focal point.

---

### 7. Make the page feel more premium through spacing

Audit the entire hero vertically and horizontally.

Check:
- header height
- content max-width
- left/right padding
- headline width
- CTA spacing
- trust statement spacing
- bottom hero padding
- relationship between hero and next section

There should be **intentional breathing room**, but not excessive empty space.

The hero should feel balanced at large desktop widths such as 1440px–1920px.

---

### 8. Add subtle conversion psychology

Do not add clutter.

The hero should immediately communicate:

**Solar → lower electricity bills → trustworthy installation → easy next step**

Without adding additional large cards or statistics, use hierarchy, copy proximity, iconography and spacing to reinforce this mental sequence.

The visitor should understand the proposition within approximately 3 seconds.

---

### 9. Responsive behavior

Do not optimize only for the screenshot.

Ensure the improved composition works across:

- 1440px desktop
- 1920px wide desktop
- 1280px laptop
- tablet
- mobile

On smaller screens:
- maintain headline readability
- prevent the solar panels from being cropped awkwardly
- preserve CTA prominence
- prevent navigation collisions
- maintain visual balance between image and content

Do not simply scale everything down.

---

### 10. Avoid these mistakes

Do NOT:
- rebuild the entire homepage
- change the logo
- change the brand colors dramatically
- replace the existing copy
- add multiple floating cards
- add excessive statistics
- add fake dashboard widgets
- add unnecessary animations
- use generic SaaS design patterns
- use excessive glassmorphism
- darken the entire image
- make the hero visually noisy
- make the house look like an ultra-luxury villa
- over-round every element
- add decorative elements that do not improve conversion

---

### 11. Micro-interactions

Keep animations restrained and premium.

Use:
- subtle CTA hover elevation
- smooth arrow movement
- slight navigation interaction
- very subtle hero entrance animation if already supported by the project

Avoid:
- large parallax effects
- aggressive zoom
- bouncing elements
- flashy transitions
- excessive motion

The site should feel **confident and expensive**, not animated for the sake of animation.

---

### 12. Final quality test

After implementation, inspect the hero as if you were seeing SolarARK for the first time.

Ask:

1. What do I notice first?
2. Can I read the headline immediately?
3. Is the solar installation clearly visible?
4. Does the CTA immediately stand out?
5. Does the hero feel trustworthy?
6. Does the image feel like a real Indian home?
7. Does the composition feel intentionally art-directed?
8. Does the bottom transition look natural?
9. Does the navigation remain secondary?
10. Does the whole section look like a premium custom-designed solar brand rather than a template?

Make iterative visual adjustments until the answer to all ten is clearly yes.

### Important implementation rule

Use the attached screenshot as the reference for the **current state**, but make design decisions based on professional visual hierarchy and conversion principles rather than blindly reproducing the screenshot.

The objective is:

**Keep the strong existing concept → remove the awkwardness → improve hierarchy → improve trust → improve conversion → make the hero feel professionally art-directed.**

Do not proceed to a new hero image in this task. First perfect the existing hero composition and interface treatment.
